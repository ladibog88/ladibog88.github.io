Sunbee Cam

Live Nest Cam — Olive-Backed Sunbirds on our Balcony

A pair of olive-backed sunbirds started building their nest on our Singapore apartment balcony. I set up a camera and built a live streaming site so family and friends could watch the nest from anywhere in the world. It runs on HLS.js for smooth adaptive streaming and Firebase for a real-time live/offline indicator.

HTML/CSS/JS HLS.js Firebase Realtime DB HLS Streaming

How It Started

Min and I noticed a pair of olive-backed sunbirds starting to build a nest on a plant on our ledge. Driven by the fear of missing out on any steps in the nesting and hatching process, we thought to set up a camera to catch all the moments.

I also wanted to share this moment with people I love without them having to come over. So I rigged up a camera, figured out live streaming, and had a working site up by evening. The nest cam became a small daily ritual — checking in on the birds, checking in on the nest, and wondering when they would start laying eggs.

It's one of those projects that reminded me that building something for a specific, real moment is deeply satisfying — more than any tutorial project ever was.

How It Works

📷

Camera captures the nest

A small camera positioned on the balcony records the sunbird nest continuously

📡

HLS stream is generated

The footage is encoded into HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) format — small chunks of video served over HTTP

🌐

HLS.js handles playback

The browser-side HLS.js library picks up the stream and plays it back smoothly with adaptive bitrate support

🔴

Firebase shows live status

A Firebase Realtime Database flag updates when the stream is active or offline — the site shows a live indicator in real time

🔗

Share via link

Anyone with the URL can watch from anywhere — no app, no login, no faff

Features

Live Video Stream

Adaptive HLS playback that adjusts quality based on the viewer's connection speed. Works on mobile and desktop.

Live / Offline Indicator

A real-time status badge powered by Firebase Realtime Database. Updates instantly when the stream goes live or offline.

No Backend Required

The site itself is fully static — no server, no backend. Firebase handles the one piece of dynamic state.

Dark Nature Theme

A dark green, nature-inspired design so the nest footage stays centre stage. Minimal chrome, maximum bird.

What I Learned

📡

HLS is Simpler Than It Looks

HTTP Live Streaming just serves small video chunks as regular files. Once I understood that, the whole protocol clicked — it's cleverly simple, which is why it works on almost every device and network.

🔴

Real-Time State Without a Backend

Firebase Realtime Database let me add a live/offline indicator without spinning up any server. Writing a single boolean to Firebase and reading it client-side felt like magic the first time it worked.

🔌

Hardware Is the Hard Part

Getting the camera positioned correctly, stable, and outputting the right format took longer than writing the entire website. Software is forgiving; physics is not.

🎨

Design for the Offline State First

Streams go down. Designing the offline experience — a graceful message rather than a broken player — was just as important as the live view. Users notice when something handles failure well.

🐦

Real Purpose Makes Better Projects

I built this to share a specific moment with specific people. That constraint made every decision easier — no feature creep, no second-guessing the scope. Build for a real reason and the project almost designs itself.
